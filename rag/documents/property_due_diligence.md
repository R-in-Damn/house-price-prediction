# Property Due Diligence: General Checklist

This document is general educational information. It is not legal advice. Verify the applicable rules, forms, fees, authorities, and deadlines for the property's state and local jurisdiction.

## Documents to verify before buying

A buyer commonly asks for the ownership or title documents, the seller's identity and authority to sell, prior sale or transfer documents, tax or assessment receipts, approved plans and permits, occupancy or completion evidence where applicable, association or maintenance records, utility records, and written disclosures about disputes, liens, encumbrances, or pending notices. The exact document set depends on the property, transaction, lender, and jurisdiction.

Ask an independent qualified professional to review the chain of title and check whether the seller can convey the interest being sold. A listing, brochure, or seller statement is not by itself proof of title or approval.

## Sale agreement checks

Read the legal description, parties, price, payment schedule, possession date, conditions precedent, default remedies, representations, inclusions and exclusions, taxes and fees, inspection rights, approval responsibilities, dispute process, termination rights, and document handover obligations. Confirm that blank spaces and schedules are completed before signing. Do not rely on a generic template when local law or the property structure requires a tailored agreement.

## RERA and project information

RERA commonly refers to a real-estate regulation framework. Project registration, disclosures, remedies, and authority processes depend on the applicable jurisdiction and the type and status of the project. A buyer should verify project and promoter information through the relevant official regulator or government portal rather than relying only on marketing material. This source does not state a universal registration threshold, deadline, or remedy.

## General registration process

A property transfer may involve agreeing the contract, completing due diligence, calculating applicable taxes and fees, arranging payment or financing, signing the required instrument, presenting identity and property documents to the relevant authority, paying required charges, and collecting the registered record. The sequence, documents, appointment process, fees, and timelines vary by jurisdiction and transaction type. Confirm the current process with the responsible land or registration authority.

## Common due-diligence steps

Confirm identity and authority, inspect the property and boundaries, compare physical conditions with approved plans, search for liens or disputes, verify taxes and service charges, check land-use and building approvals, review association obligations, confirm utilities and access, obtain an independent valuation where useful, and keep a written record of searches and professional advice. A buyer should not treat this general checklist as proof that a particular property is legally clear.
